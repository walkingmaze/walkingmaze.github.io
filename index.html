A bit about how I got here

In 2025, I discovered surfing, and it gave me a new mindset, in a way. Before that, I found comfort in trying to learn web development and then branching out into automation testing. The first journey was completely solo; for the second, I found mentors.

Both things require patience, focus, and grinding—conditions I wish I could harness more consistently. This is the flow state I'm chasing, and I'm sharing some of the ride here along the way.

The short version

A journey of 1,445 characters

But why did I start with code? What was the motivation? Lines of code creating a galaxy I could influence like a wizard with a wand.

Not really. Maybe it was the promise of immersing oneself in the writing of the code. I’ve always liked writing and I journal a lot.

And it turned out, code offered a different kind of immersion, it scratches a part of your brain you didn’t know existed—or needed scratching.

So what's the journey have looked so far?

First came the Reddit threads, then freeCodeCamp. Then came The Odin Project.

Then came the doubt. I didn’t have the right background, the competition is tough.

Then another thought: perhaps I could move in the same direction through a different opening. Pivot towards test automation. Put down roots somewhere nearby and see what grew.

The idea of testing came from Per Anhalter durch die IT-Branche book, which described it as a possible entry point for a Quereinsteiger.

And then, of course: AI will sweep us all away anyway. I’d better learn to ride the wave rather than be swallowed by it. That reminded me of lines from this poem:

“…swallow the tide, become the water, they will call it drowning you’ll call it birth, dancing in the palm of God on another earth.”

— Amy Leon

Long story short: I still need to finish The Odin Project, because that calculator looks hässlich. Meanwhile, my hands are full of projects, because I’m not stopping:

“If one door is closed, break a window anyway.”

— Internet
