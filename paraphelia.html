Glitter Up the Dark

The bands that sprung up in Bikini Kill’s wake furthered their commitment to ferocity over precision. Wolfe and her band, Bratmobile, who befriended Bikini Kill and moved with them to DC, explicitly stated the importance of learning the ropes and playing “badly” in public to show potential women musicians that everybody starts somewhere.

— Sasha Geffen, Glitter Up the Dark: How Pop Music Created the Space for LGBT Expression

I think it's really good for bands to go out when they are not ready. Because then, as you do get a grasp on your instruments, people see you in a continuum, as opposed to you just jumping out of nowhere, which is what I always thought: The boy comes out of the womb with a screaming Led Zeppelin guitar, and I feel like I'll never know how to do that. ´

— Molly Neuman
